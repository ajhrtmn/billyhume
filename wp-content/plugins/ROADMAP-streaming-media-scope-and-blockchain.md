# ROADMAP — Streaming media scope and transferable digital licenses

Design pass only. Nothing in this document is built. Both ideas here started as open-ended thoughts floated by AJ, deliberately deferred to a standalone research pass rather than answered off the cuff. **AJ has since made both calls**: video/live/long-audio are worth scoping (not just long-audio), and the blockchain direction is specifically a transferable digital license — a real, ownable, resellable license per copy of a track, the way a physical record or CD naturally has exactly one owner at a time — not a speculative NFT marketplace or a crypto payment rail. This revision scopes both decisions honestly, including the parts of the license idea that are harder than they first sound.

## Part 1 — "Streaming should consider all streamable media"

### 1.1 What bh-streaming actually is today

Every assumption below is read directly from the plugin, not inferred:

- **`bhs_track` is the only real content post type**, and it's audio-shaped by construction: `supports => ['title']` only, no video-specific fields anywhere in the schema ([class-post-types.php:40-54](bh-streaming/includes/class-post-types.php#L40-L54)). `bhs_release` (album/EP grouping) and `bhs_playlist` (ordered set) both just group `bhs_track` posts — neither has any media-type awareness of its own.
- **Local import hard-rejects non-audio at two layers**: `media_handle_upload()`'s own MIME sniffing, then a second explicit check — `strpos($mime, 'audio/') !== 0` deletes the attachment and 400s ([class-import.php:70-74](bh-streaming/includes/class-import.php#L70-L74)). This isn't a UI label that happens to say "audio," it's an enforced gate.
- **Feed import/export is built on the podcast RSS convention specifically** — "RSS 2.0 + the iTunes/Podcasting-2.0 enclosure convention" ([class-feeds.php:6-16](bh-streaming/includes/class-feeds.php#L6-L16)). That standard has a video variant (`<enclosure type="video/...">` is technically legal RSS-with-enclosures), but nothing in `class-feeds.php` was written expecting one — the whole doc-comment framing is "Funkwhale channel," "podcast app," audio vocabulary throughout.
- **The player (`class-player.php`) is an audio player.** Its own React/JS harness, deep-linking, and search-result cards are all built around a scrubber-and-playlist UI, not a video viewport. Not fully audited line-by-line here, but nothing in its `init()`/search-provider code suggests otherwise.
- **The rights/identity layer is audio-specific by name and by mechanism**: `_bhs_audio_id`, `_bhs_audio_hash` (sha1 of the audio file's bytes — [class-audio-hash.php:34-53](bh-streaming/includes/class-audio-hash.php#L34-L53)), and ISRC ([class-isrc.php](bh-streaming/includes/class-isrc.php)) — ISRC is *specifically* a sound-recording identifier standard; it has no meaning for video at all. A video track would need a different identifier scheme entirely (ISAN is the video analog), not a renamed field.

Conclusion: "audio-only" isn't a UI limitation sitting on top of a general media schema — it's baked into the CPT support flags, the upload gate, the feed convention, the hash/rights fields, and the identifier standard. There's no single flag to flip.

### 1.2 What "all streamable media" could actually mean — three different asks, not one

**A. Long-form spoken audio (audiobooks, longer podcast episodes).** This is barely an expansion at all — it's the *same* medium (audio), just longer. `bhs_track` already supports arbitrary-length audio files; nothing in the schema imposes a length ceiling. The only real gap is UX: a player built around song-length scrubbing (seconds-to-a-few-minutes) doesn't obviously handle "resume where I left off in a 3-hour audiobook" or chapter markers well, and that's a `class-player.php` UI feature, not a schema change. **Cost: small.** This barely deserves the word "expansion" — it's closer to a player feature request.

**B. Video.** This is the expansion AJ's phrasing ("all streamable media") most naturally points to, and it's also the expensive one:
- New CPT or a real content-type field on `bhs_track` (the latter risks becoming exactly the kind of premature abstraction this ecosystem's own docs (e.g. `ROADMAP-lms-v3.md`'s stated anti-over-engineering discipline) warn against — don't generalize `bhs_track` into "any media" if 95% of the schema stays audio-specific).
- A real video player component — not a small lift; scrubbing, resolution/quality selection, and captions are a different UI problem than an audio waveform.
- Storage and bandwidth cost changes materially. WordPress's media library will happily store a video upload, but self-hosted video at any real scale (this ecosystem's entire posture — see bh-streaming's local-first design) means real disk and real egress cost for a solo musician's install, not a hypothetical.
- The rights layer (`_bhs_audio_hash`, ISRC) needs a parallel video path — sha1-of-file-bytes duplicate detection ports over fine conceptually, but ISRC does not; a video analog (ISAN, or nothing yet) would need its own honest-placeholder treatment same as `class-isrc.php` already does for audio.
- The feed convention (podcast RSS) has a video-enclosure form, but `class-feeds.php`'s whole framing — Funkwhale, podcast apps — assumes audio consumers on the other end; opening this to video feeds means re-examining whether the "open standard, not one platform's API" principle still holds (video RSS/enclosure feeds are much less commonly implemented than audio podcast feeds in the wild).

**Cost: large — closer to a second plugin (or a major-version rearchitecture of this one) than a feature addition.**

**C. Live streaming.** A fundamentally different technical problem from A and B — not "a track with a different file," but a persistent connection, a streaming protocol (RTMP/HLS/WebRTC), and infrastructure this plugin has zero of today. Nothing in the current codebase is a stepping stone toward this the way local-import audio is a stepping stone toward long-form audio. **Cost: largest, and closest to "different product," not "expanded feature."**

### 1.3 AJ's call: scope all three — phased, not simultaneous

AJ wants video and live scoped alongside long-form audio, not deferred until a specific use case forces the question. Given the real cost gap between A and (B, C) from 1.2, the honest way to do that without over-building is a phased sequence where each phase is independently shippable and nothing in an earlier phase is thrown away by a later one:

**Phase 1 — Long-form audio (small, do first).** Player UX for resume-position and chapter markers on longer audio files. No schema change (`bhs_track` already stores arbitrary-length audio). This phase should ship on its own regardless of what happens with B/C — it's cheap and already half-supported.

**STATUS UPDATE (2026-07-26): Phase 1 is fully shipped, not an open recommendation.** This section was stale — someone already built it in full: `bh-streaming/includes/class-chapters.php` (`BHS_Chapters`) gives `bhs_track` a "Chapters" metabox (parses `mm:ss Label` lines into `_bhs_chapters` JSON), a REST route persisting per-user resume position (`_bhs_resume_{track_id}` user meta), and `class-api.php`/`assets/js/player.js` wire both ends-to-end (clickable chapter markers, auto-seek to the saved resume position on load, throttled progress-save). Registered in `bh-streaming.php`. Live-verified in a real browser this pass (real track, real chapter lines including a deliberately malformed one to confirm it's silently skipped, real player) and covered by `class-test-suite.php::run_chapters_tests()` (line-parsing/sorting, resume read/write round-trip). Nothing here is still open.

**STATUS UPDATE (2026-07-26): Phases 2 and 3 are both shipped too — this whole Part is now fully built, not an open recommendation.** AJ's own answers to the three open questions below (standalone video content, not tied to tracks/releases; turnkey scale options for both video and live; two-way interactive for live, not simpler one-way) directly shaped what got built: a new peer plugin **`bh-video`** (`bhv_video` CPT — genuinely separate from `bhs_track`, exactly as Phase 2 recommended, not a generalized/forced-medium-agnostic `bhs_track` — REST catalog API, admin upload metabox, `[bh_video]` browse/playback SPA, chapters/resume-position ported from `BHS_Chapters`) and a new peer plugin **`bh-live`** (two-way interactive live streaming behind a `BHL_StreamEngine` interface with two real implementations — self-hosted Owncast, free but needs its own server, and Cloudflare Stream Live, managed/metered/video-only — plus a `BHL_HostProvisioner` interface with a real Fly.io Machines implementation that deploys/destroys the Owncast box directly from `OUS_MediaWizard`, and a `BHL_Chat` interface with three real options: Owncast's bundled chat, a free polling-based `BHL_PollingChat`, and a real-time `BHL_WorkersChat` via a Cloudflare Worker + Durable Object). Both plugins have full `OUS_TestRunner` coverage; the Fly.io/Cloudflare/Workers request shapes were verified against each provider's real documented API via request interception (no live paid account available this session) — worth a real dry-run against an actual account before relying on any of the three in production. See `STATUS.md` for the full detail.

**Phase 2 — Video (superseded by the status update above, kept for the original reasoning).** Scope as a genuinely separate CPT (`bhs_video` or similar), not a generalized `bhs_track` — per 1.2's point, forcing 95%-audio-specific fields (`_bhs_audio_hash`, ISRC) to pretend to be medium-agnostic is exactly the premature abstraction this ecosystem's docs warn against elsewhere. Concretely needs, before any code:
- A real storage/bandwidth budget conversation with AJ — self-hosted video at scale is a materially different hosting cost than audio, and that number should be known *before* committing, not discovered after artists start uploading.
- A video player component (separate build from `class-player.php`'s audio scrubber).
- A parallel rights/hash path: sha1-duplicate-detection ports over directly, but ISRC does not — video's identifier analog (ISAN) needs its own `class-isrc.php`-style honest-placeholder treatment, or an explicit decision to skip identifier issuance for video v1 and add it later.
- A decision on whether video should be reachable via the feed-import/export mechanism (podcast-RSS video enclosures exist but are far less commonly implemented in the wild than audio ones — this may be a "local-upload only for v1" feature, with feed support deferred).

**Phase 3 — Live streaming (superseded by the status update above, kept for the original reasoning).** The most different from what exists today — a persistent connection and a streaming protocol (RTMP/HLS/WebRTC), not a file. Should be scoped only after Phase 2 ships and its real player/infra lessons are known; scoping it now, on paper, ahead of two entire other phases, risks the plan drifting stale before it's ever touched. Recommend treating Phase 3 as "next roadmap doc, written after Phase 2 ships," not "designed now."

**Open questions for AJ (answered — see the status update above):**
1. Standalone video content, not tied to tracks/releases — `bh-video` is its own catalog.
2. Turnkey scale options for both — `OUS_MediaWizard`'s storage/CDN wizard covers video hosting; `bh-live`'s `BHL_HostProvisioner`/Fly.io integration covers live.
3. Two-way interactive, not simpler one-way — `bh-live`'s whole `BHL_Chat` abstraction exists because of this answer.

## Part 2 — Ledger-anchored proof of purchase (not transferable ownership, not resale)

AJ's clarification narrows this significantly from the earlier "transferable license" framing: the actual goal is **proof of purchase that can't be disputed or revoked**, giving the buyer confidence their purchase record is permanent, and giving the artist a durable, tamper-evident log of sales — a metrics/audit trail, not a resale or transfer mechanism. This is a smaller, more clearly-scoped feature than the previous draft, and it turns out to interact directly — and in some tension — with fraud/entitlement code that already exists in `bh-monetization-woo`.

### 2.1 What already exists, and the direct tension this idea runs into

`bh-monetization-woo` already has a real, deliberate answer to "what happens to access after a refund or chargeback," and it's the opposite of "can't be revoked":

- `BHM_Entitlements::on_order_reversed()` fires on both `woocommerce_order_status_refunded` and `woocommerce_order_status_cancelled`, and its own docblock states the reasoning plainly: *"A refunded or cancelled order must actually TAKE BACK whatever it granted — otherwise a chargeback ... leaves the entitlement standing forever with the artist eating the loss AND the fraudster keeping what they 'bought.'"* ([class-entitlements.php:16-26](bh-monetization-woo/includes/class-entitlements.php#L16-L26))
- `BHM_Fraud::track_refund_pattern()` exists specifically to catch "buy → stream/download → refund → repeat" abuse, flagging repeated refunds from the same account for admin review ([class-fraud.php:13-19](bh-monetization-woo/includes/class-fraud.php#L13-L19)).

So the platform's current, intentional design is: a chargeback/refund *always* revokes access, because the alternative is a well-known fraud vector. AJ's ask — "purchased media can't be disputed and revoked" — has to be reconciled with this, not built as if it doesn't exist. The honest resolution is a scope distinction:

- **What a ledger CAN honestly provide**: an independently-verifiable, tamper-evident record that *the purchase transaction itself occurred* — price, timestamp, buyer, track — that survives even if this site's own database were lost, altered, or a payment processor's own records were disputed. That's real, valuable evidence in a genuine dispute (e.g., a bank flags a legitimate charge as suspicious and the buyer needs to prove they authorized it; a processor's records are incomplete; the site itself needs to defend against a chargeback with clean documentation of what was actually delivered).
- **What it can NOT honestly provide**: a guarantee that access is restored or maintained *regardless of what the payment processor decides*. If a card issuer reverses a charge, the money is gone from the artist's side regardless of what any ledger says — a ledger entry doesn't move money back. Building this feature as "once anchored, access can never be taken away" would directly reopen the exact abuse pattern `BHM_Fraud`/`BHM_Entitlements` were built to close (pay fraudulently, get flagged by anchored "proof" as untouchable, keep access, artist eats the loss). That would make this feature actively harmful to the artists it's meant to protect, not neutral.

The feature that's actually being asked for, stated honestly: **immutable evidence of what happened, not immutable enforcement of access.** That's a meaningfully smaller and safer claim than "can't be revoked," and it's the version worth building.

### 2.2 A concrete, minimal shape

1. **Purchase record, anchored at time of sale**: hash of `{track_id, content_hash (reuse `_bhs_audio_hash`), buyer_id (or pseudonymous), price, order_id, timestamp}`. Anchor the hash to a public ledger (OpenTimestamps-style — no wallet, no cryptocurrency held or spent by artist or buyer) at the moment `woocommerce_order_status_completed` fires (the same hook `BHM_Entitlements::on_order_completed` already listens to — [class-entitlements.php:24](bh-monetization-woo/includes/class-entitlements.php#L24) area).
2. **On refund/chargeback**: `BHM_Entitlements::on_order_reversed()` keeps doing exactly what it does today — access is revoked, full stop, no exception carved out for anchored purchases. The anchored record is **not** consulted by the access-control path at all; it exists purely as an evidence artifact. This is the load-bearing design choice that keeps the feature from reopening the fraud vector in 2.1.
3. **What the anchored record is actually for, concretely**:
   - **Buyer-facing**: a "Verify this purchase" page/receipt showing the anchored proof, so a buyer disputing a chargeback with their own bank, or simply wanting a permanent personal record ("I really did buy this on this date, here's independent proof"), has something no single company's database outage or shutdown can erase.
   - **Artist-facing**: exactly what AJ called it — a metrics log. A running, tamper-evident sales ledger per track/release that isn't just a number in this site's own WooCommerce dashboard, which is useful the same way this ecosystem's `ROADMAP-federated-metrics.md` already argues real numbers should be independently reproducible, not just self-reported.
4. **Revocation gets recorded too, not hidden**: when `on_order_reversed()` fires, anchor a second, linked record — "purchase X was reversed at time Y" — rather than silently deleting the original entry from view. This keeps the artist's log honest (a reversed sale shouldn't quietly vanish from the count as if it never happened) and keeps the buyer's "proof of purchase" from being a claim that a reversal contradicts — the ledger shows the true full history, purchase and reversal both, which is more trustworthy than a log that only ever shows successes.

### 2.3 What still shouldn't be built

- **Access restoration triggered by the anchored record.** As established in 2.1, this is the one thing that would make the feature actively harmful — it would hand fraud a documented bypass around `BHM_Fraud`'s own abuse detection.
- **Any resale/transfer mechanism.** Explicitly not what AJ is asking for now — this is a receipt, not a deed. If ownership transfer becomes a real ask later, it's a separate, larger feature (the earlier draft of this doc scoped what that would take, if it comes up again).
- **On-chain storage of the audio, buyer PII, or full order details.** Only a hash is anchored; the actual purchase record stays in this site's own database (WooCommerce order + a `bhm`/`bhs`-side receipt row) same as today. Anchoring buyer-identifying information on a public, permanent ledger would be a real privacy problem — worse than doing nothing, since it can't be taken back down. A pseudonymous or hashed buyer reference (not a name or email) keeps the public ledger entry meaningless to anyone without this site's own database to cross-reference it against.

### 2.4 Honest verdict

This is a real, well-scoped, buildable feature once framed as "tamper-evident evidence trail," and it's a better fit for this ecosystem's actual values (verifiable, anti-middleman, protects the small artist from unaccountable platform/processor behavior) than either the earlier timestamp-only idea or the transferable-license idea — because it has a concrete answer to "what problem does this solve" (disputes and processor opacity) without requiring the harder, still-unresolved product decisions the transfer/resale framing needed (playback gating, resale royalties, wallet infrastructure). The one thing that has to be gotten right from day one is the boundary in 2.1/2.2: anchored proof is evidence, never an override of the existing fraud/entitlement logic. Get that backwards and the feature actively makes the fraud problem this ecosystem already solved worse, not better.

**Recommendation:** worth building, scoped as above — it's a genuinely small addition (one anchoring call added to two existing hooks, a receipt-verification page, no changes to `BHM_Entitlements`'s or `BHM_Fraud`'s actual logic) rather than a new subsystem. The main remaining decision is anchoring-service choice (see 2.2, OpenTimestamps or equivalent) and whether the "artist metrics log" view lives in `bh-streaming`'s admin, `bh-monetization-woo`'s, or `bh-crm`'s (it's arguably a `bh-crm`-style activity feed more than a streaming-plugin feature, per this ecosystem's existing cross-plugin convention of doing person/activity views there).

**STATUS UPDATE (2026-07-26): this whole section is shipped, not an open recommendation.** Someone already built it exactly as scoped above: `bh-monetization-woo/includes/class-purchase-ledger.php` (`BHM_PurchaseLedger`) hooks `woocommerce_order_status_completed`/`refunded`/`cancelled` as fully separate callbacks from `BHM_Entitlements` (never touching the access-control path — the 2.1 boundary holds in practice, confirmed live: a real refund revoked the entitlement AND still left the original purchase record standing, undisturbed, exactly as this section describes), writes a SHA-256 hash of the purchase record, and hands it to `class-anchoring.php` (`BHM_Anchoring`), which really does submit it to three live OpenTimestamps calendar servers via `OUS_Jobs` (async, never blocking checkout) and later polls for Bitcoin block-confirmation. Live-verified this pass end-to-end (real order → real ledger row → real anchoring job → real refund → linked reversal row → verify page reflecting both). Only the third-party-integration side (2.5's YouTube Content ID note, 2.6's PRO-report export) remains genuinely open, both explicitly flagged there as "not scoped yet," not stale.

### 2.5 YouTube Content ID as a future metadata source — flagged, not scoped

AJ raised this in passing: pairing this with YouTube Content ID could surface metadata beyond what's designed above. Worth being precise about what Content ID actually is before treating it as a data source, because it's commonly misunderstood: it's YouTube's own proprietary audio/video fingerprinting system for detecting matches to a rights-holder's registered catalog across everything uploaded to YouTube — not a public API a small site can freely query, and not something anchored to any blockchain (it's a closed, centralized system, arguably the opposite of this ecosystem's stated anti-middleman posture). Two very different integration shapes hide under "integrate with Content ID," worth naming so a future pass doesn't conflate them:

- **AJ's own catalog gets registered as a Content ID rights holder** (via a YouTube Content ID partner / MCN, since direct Content ID enrollment isn't self-serve for small catalogs) — this would surface real match data: where else on YouTube this music appears, sync/reuse patterns, potential unauthorized use. That's genuinely valuable "other metadata," but it's an institutional-partnership step outside this codebase, exactly the same shape as `class-isrc.php`'s own honest framing of real ISRC issuance ("needs Own Ur Shit to be a registered ISRC registrant ... a real institutional application, not something this class can do" — [class-isrc.php:9-11](bh-streaming/includes/class-isrc.php#L9-L11)). The right move now is the same one already taken for ISRC: don't build against it speculatively; note it as a real future door, and if/when AJ actually pursues Content ID partnership, that's the point to design the concrete field/sync shape against real API access, not before.
- **Pulling public YouTube video metadata for a track** (view counts, upload matches found by ad hoc search) is a much smaller, already-possible thing via the standard YouTube Data API — but it's a different, weaker feature than true Content ID matching, and conflating the two in a pitch to AJ later would overstate what's actually being built.

No action recommended here beyond this note — there's no concrete request yet, just a door worth remembering exists. If it becomes a real priority, it deserves its own short scoping pass once it's clear which of the two shapes above (or something else) is actually wanted.

### 2.6 PRO/registration-organization integration — the real opportunity is a report, not an API

bh-streaming already has `class-pro-wizard.php`, and it's explicit about why it's "honestly a THINNER tool" than the plugin's other wizards: *"A PRO has no public membership-verification API — ASCAP/BMI don't expose 'is this IPI number real' as a service, and SESAC/GMR are invitation-only and don't even have a self-serve signup page to link to"* ([class-pro-wizard.php:4-16](bh-streaming/includes/class-pro-wizard.php#L4-L16)). It currently stores one thing: the artist's own self-reported PRO/IPI affiliation (`bhs_pro_affiliation`), with "no 'test connection' step exists here because there's nothing this code can verify." That constraint is real and doesn't change — PROs are not going to expose an API for a small self-hosted plugin to push data into.

But that constraint points at what actually *would* be useful here, and it connects directly to Section 2's proof-of-purchase/metrics ledger: **PROs (and, more directly, mechanical-royalty bodies like the MLC) generally rely on artists/publishers to self-report usage they don't otherwise see** — direct-site sales and streams outside the PRO's own tracked channels (radio, big-platform streaming) are exactly the kind of activity a PRO has no visibility into unless the rights holder tells them. So the genuinely useful integration isn't a live API call — it's **an export**: a report generator that takes the anchored purchase/play ledger from Section 2 and formats it into whatever a given PRO or the MLC actually asks for on a royalty claim/registration form (composition title, ISWC if registered, play/sale counts, dates, verifiable via the anchored records if ever questioned).

This is a small, honest, buildable feature once 2.2's ledger exists — it doesn't require solving anything PROs haven't solved themselves, it just saves the artist from manually compiling that report from raw WooCommerce/streaming-stats data by hand. It should be scoped as a follow-on to Section 2, not before it, since it depends on the ledger existing first.

**Open questions for AJ:**
1. Is there a specific PRO/registration body already in mind (ASCAP/BMI's own royalty-claim process, the MLC for mechanicals, or something else), or is this a general "give artists paperwork PROs will accept" idea? The concrete report format differs meaningfully between them.
2. Should this be a manual "export report" action an artist triggers when they need it, or does it belong as a recurring feature (e.g. a quarterly auto-generated summary) alongside `class-pro-wizard.php`'s existing admin page?
